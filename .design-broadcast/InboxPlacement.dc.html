Design note · answering "can it go to Primary?"

Primary vs Promotions — what's actually in your control

Gmail's tab choice is made per recipient, not per message. Two people can receive the identical email and see it in different tabs, because Gmail weighs that person's own history with the sender.

So there is no server-side setting that forces Primary. But the mode you send in genuinely changes the odds — and one lever moves it far more than the rest.

Broadcast

→ Promotions

Newsletters, product news, anything the recipient could reasonably opt out of.

Carries, by law and by policy

•List-Unsubscribe + one-click header
•Feedback-ID — added by the sending platform
•Visible unsubscribe footer

Those three are exactly what Gmail reads as "bulk". Removing them to chase Primary trades a Promotions placement for a real spam-folder risk — and above ~5,000/day Gmail requires them outright.

Operational notice

→ Primary, usually

Seat renewals, account changes, session details — things the recipient needs whether or not they want news.

Sent as transactional

•No unsubscribe — this mail isn't optional
•Plain, letter-shaped: no hero image, no buttons
•Addressed to the person by name, one link at most

Dressing marketing up as operational is what gets domains blocked. The mode is recorded on every send and shown in the history report.

The one lever that actually moves people to Primary

Recipient behaviour beats every header. If someone replies, stars the message, drags it to Primary, or adds the sender to their contacts, Gmail learns — and future mail from suchismita.sen@imotara.com goes to that person's Primary tab from then on.

Ask once, in the first send

One plain line — "reply with a word, or drag this to your main inbox, and future notes will land there." It works, and it only needs saying once.

Make replies possible

Send from a real monitored mailbox, never no-reply@. You already do — replies reach Suchismita directly.

Stay consistent

Same From address every time. Reputation and per-recipient learning both attach to the address, so rotating senders throws it away.

Content signals — worth a little, not a lot

Pushes toward Primary

↑Mostly text, few or no images
↑One link, not eight
↑The recipient's own name in the greeting
↑A subject that reads like a person wrote it
↑Single-column, letter-like layout

Pushes toward Promotions

↓Hero images and big call-to-action buttons
↓Multi-column or card layouts
↓Offer language — free, save, limited, exclusive
↓Many recipients in one run
↓Tracking pixels — which Imotara doesn't use anyway

Being straight about the ceiling: a genuine broadcast to thousands will mostly sit in Promotions no matter how it is written, and that is fine — it is the inbox, people read it there, and it is not spam. The composer scores these signals so the choice is visible, not so the number can be gamed.
