# The language videos

**The one thing competitors can't copy.** Twenty videos on the channel and not one in Hindi,
Bengali or Tamil — while "mental health app" is an unwinnable search and
"মানসিক স্বাস্থ্য অ্যাপ" is close to uncontested.

---

## Two decisions that come before any script

**Post to Facebook first, YouTube second.** Measured 16 Oct: Facebook reels pull **100–270 views
each**; YouTube Shorts on the same channel pull **0–8**. Same content, roughly thirty times the
reach. The Facebook Page is the stronger asset and nobody had noticed.

**The title must be in the native script.** That is the searchable asset — the entire point. A
transliterated title ("Bangla-y kotha bolun") is searched by almost nobody;
**"বাংলায় কথা বলুন"** is what a Bengali speaker actually types. Description in that language too.

---

## ⛔ What I will not do, and why

**I am not writing the spoken words in eleven languages.** I can produce text in all of them;
I cannot *verify* it, and the owner has already corrected my Bengali once — `লাগছে` takes `আমার`
⇒ [[feedback_bengali_test_strings_must_be_natural]].

On a mental-health product, a sentence that is grammatically fine but emotionally wrong is worse
than no video. "I hear you" rendered as something stiff or clinical does more damage than silence,
and the viewer has no way to know it was a translation error.

**So: the structure, the shot list and the brief are below. The words come from someone who
speaks the language.** That is ten minutes of their time per video, not an hour.

---

## The format — 45 to 60 seconds

Same five beats every time. The repetition is the point: it becomes recognisable.

| Beat | Seconds | What is on screen |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The feeling** | 0–8 | A person, alone, in an ordinary Indian room. No dialogue. |
| **2. The sentence** | 8–18 | They type ONE real sentence into Imotara, in their language. Screen recording, real app. |
| **3. The reply** | 18–35 | Imotara's actual reply appears. **Unedited.** Let it be read. |
| **4. The turn** | 35–50 | Their face changes. Small. Not a transformation — a breath. |
| **5. The line** | 50–60 | One card: *"Imotara speaks your language."* + the app icon. |

⛔ **No voice-over.** ⛔ **No music swell at beat 4.** ⛔ **No "download now".** The product's
whole claim is that it does not perform at you; a video that performs contradicts it.

🔑 **Beat 3 is the video.** Everything else is setup. Do not shorten the reply to fit — if it is
too long, pick a shorter opening sentence in beat 2.

## The brief for your native speaker

Hand them this. It is the whole job.

> We are making a 50-second video showing someone talking to Imotara in **<language>**.
> We need three things from you, in natural spoken **<language>** — the way someone would
> actually say it to a friend, not textbook phrasing.
>
> **1. One sentence the person types.** Something ordinary and real that someone might
> genuinely say at the end of a hard day. Not dramatic. Not a crisis. Examples of the register
> we want, in English: *"I'm tired and I don't know why."* · *"Everyone's fine, I'm just
> quiet today."* · *"I couldn't sleep again."*
>
> **2. Check Imotara's reply.** We will run your sentence through the real app and show the real
> answer. Read it and tell us honestly: does it sound like a person, or like a translation?
> If it sounds stiff, say so — that is a product bug and we want to know.
>
> **3. One closing line** meaning *"Imotara speaks your language."*
>
> ⛔ Please do not make it sound like an advertisement. If it sounds like marketing, it is wrong.

🔑 **Point 2 is worth more than the video.** Eleven native speakers reading real replies is the
best language-quality audit this product could get, and it costs nothing extra.
