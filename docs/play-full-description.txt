Imotara — a private space to say how you actually feel

Most people don't want advice. They want to say the thing out loud to someone who won't flinch, change the subject, or tell the family.

Imotara is that. You type or speak; it listens, asks one good question rather than three, and remembers what mattered last time you talked. It won't diagnose you, won't tell you to think positive, and won't panic if you say something frightening.

Talk in the language you think in

Imotara speaks 22 languages, and eleven of them are Indian: Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Gujarati, Punjabi, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia and Urdu — alongside English, Arabic, Hebrew, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese and Indonesian.

Not translated English. Ordinary conversational register, the way people actually speak. Most of us feel things in our first language and only translate afterwards, and the translation is where the feeling gets lost.

You can speak instead of typing, hands-free, and Imotara can read its replies back to you.

What you can do here

Say what happened, and be heard. Keep a mood timeline, and see how a week actually went rather than how you remember it. Breathe, when the thinking won't stop. Look back at a conversation that mattered. Use it on a plane, on a train, with no signal — replies work offline.

Your conversations stay yours

Imotara is local-first. What you write stays on your device unless you switch cloud sync on yourself. No ads. No engagement algorithm. No social feed. Nothing sold to anyone, ever. You can export or delete everything from one screen.

Please read this part

Imotara is not therapy, not a doctor, and not a crisis service. It is a companion for reflection, and it will say so itself rather than pretend otherwise.
